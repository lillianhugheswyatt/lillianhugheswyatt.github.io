<h2 style="font-size: 35px;">
        Controlled creation of quantum defects in diamond
  </h2>

  <img
    class="thrust-figure-left"  
    src="{{ '/assets/img/Thrust1_website.png' | relative_url }}"
    alt="Diamond spin systems">
      
  <p>
    We will create group IV spin defects in diamond with controlled coherence, interactions, and spatial localization for applications in quantum communication. By engineering not only the defects but also their local diamond environment, we aim to suppress spectral diffusion and enable opportunities for faster, higher-fidelity entanglement distribution.
    </p>
    <p>
      Vacancy-related centers in diamond involving group IV elements, e.g., silicon (SiV), germanium (GeV), tin (SnV), and lead (PbV), have emerged as promising spin–photon interfaces due to their split-vacancy structure which yields reduced sensitivity to electric field noise, narrow zero-phonon lines with strong emission, and efficient, coherent optical access to spin degrees of freedom.
      However, formation of group IV centers typically relies on the stochastic and uncontrolled process of ion implantation, which causes lattice damage including paramagnetic defects, charge traps, and strain, and ultimately results in degraded optical and spin properties.
       Incorporation of group IV color centers via doping during CVD will overcome the limits of implantation and enable unparalleled control over defect properties and configurations, as similarly demonstrated in our previous work with nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers (Figure illustrates depth confinement achieved with nitrogen delta doping).
      We will use a variety of synthesis-based tuning knobs to achieve successful doping of group IV color centers in diamond with precise depth confinement while also improving creation efficiency and charge state stability.
      
    </p>
  </div>

<div class="thrust-section">
  
  <h2 style="font-size: 35px;">
    Frontier superconducting heterostructures based on MgB<sub>2</sub>
  </h2>

  <img
    class="thrust-figure-right" 
    src="{{ '/assets/img/Thrust2_website_2.png' | relative_url }}"
    alt="Atomic layer processing">
      
  <p>
       We will pioneer superconducting junctions based on high-Tc materials such as MgB<sub>2</sub>, leveraging atomic layer processing to create precise tunnel barriers and unlock device operation at higher frequencies and temperatures.
    </p>
    <p>
The majority of superconducting qubit technologies rely on aluminum-based Josephson junctions, which face two key materials limitations: the relatively small superconducting gap of Al restricts operating frequency and temperature, and uncontrolled amorphous oxide barriers and interfaces host two-level systems (TLS) that introduce dielectric loss and noise that limits qubit coherence.
      We hypothesize that integrating the minimally explored high-gap superconductor MgB<sub>2</sub> with atomic-scale control of barrier thickness, composition, and interfacial structure will enable a new class of high-performance superconducting junctions operating beyond the limits of conventional Al-based devices.
      MgB<sub>2</sub> will form the ground plane of the superconductor-insulator-superconductor (SIS) structure shown in the Figure.
      We will then use atomic layer processing to create the ultrathin insulating barrier and top superconducting electrode, correlating materials structure and composition with low-temperature electrical characterization of the SIS devices.
      
    </p>
  </div>

<div class="thrust-section">

<h2 style="font-size: 35px;"> Monolithic hybrid quantum systems in diamond</h2>

  <img
      class="thrust-figure-left"  
      src="{{ '/assets/img/Thrust2_website.png' | relative_url }}"
      alt="Hybrid quantum systems">

  <p>
      We will create a unified materials platform for coherent spin–superconductor coupling and opportunities for novel hybrid quantum systems by achieving spatially-defined superconductivity in diamond through precision boron doping.
    </p>
  <p>
      Coupling superconducting circuits with solid-state spin defects could enable long-lived quantum memories and coherent microwave-to-optical interfaces, addressing critical challenges in quantum information storage and long-distance communication. 
    We hypothesize that boron-doping-induced superconductivity in diamond can be harnessed to embed a superconducting circuit very close to a spin defect and achieve strong, coherent coupling while also eliminating lossy interfaces associated with dissimilar materials stacks.
    By spatially defining superconducting and insulating regions through boron doping during CVD, our bottom-up approach will replace heterogeneous interfaces with doping-defined electronic transitions.
    We anticipate that this monolithic integration platform will unlock numerous exciting possibilities for scalable and coherent hybrid quantum technologies.
  </p>
