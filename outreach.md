I seek to build an inclusive, collaborative environment that empowers students to tackle outstanding challenges at the intersection of materials and quantum science and share in the excitement of discovery that continually drives my own research and learning.
Mentoring students in research is my utmost passion, and this is ultimately why I am pursuing an academic career.
My main goals are to foster critical thinking, curiosity, and a sense of purpose in my students and mentees. 
My pedagogical philosophy has been shaped predominantly by my experiences in mentoring five undergraduates research students, leading outreach activities including a cleanroom training class for community students seeking jobs in the local microfabrication industry, and serving as a teaching assistant and reader for several courses. 

## Teaching Experience
I helped teach a microfabrication/cleanroom training course at UCSB for local, underrepresented students (from community college or unrelated workforces) looking to get into the semiconductor or quantum industry but who had not had an opportunity to gain cleanroom fabrication skills. This NSF-funded program, called [CC-PRIME](https://www.cnsi.ucsb.edu/programs/cc-prime) (Central coast partnership for regional/industry-focused micro/nanotechnology education), features a week of hands-on cleanroom laboratory skills training for cohorts of 10 students twice a year. It was a rewarding experience to see students become so excited about microfabrication, develop confidence in new skills, and eventually even land jobs in local industry. 

My other teaching experiences include serving as a teaching assistant for introductory and graduate engineering quantum mechanics classes at UCSB.

## Outreach
I am dedicated to improving representation in science, and I hope to be a voice for scientists from all backgrounds in my future career. At UCSB, I served as president of the Materials Student Association (MSA) and a member of our department’s Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion committee.

I also led a team to develop [Quantum Kits](https://quantumfoundry.ucsb.edu/education/k12) of outreach materials for the UCSB Quantum Foundry to bring quantum science lessons and activities into local middle schools. I went into the classrooms and worked directly with a diverse community of students, inspiring them to have confidence to ask questions and experiment with new problems, even when the topic is as complicated as quantum physics.
